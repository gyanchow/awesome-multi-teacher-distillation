# How to Read Multi-Teacher Distillation: From Offline KD to MOPD

[中文](guide_zh-CN.md) · [GitHub repository](https://github.com/gyanchow/awesome-multi-teacher-distillation) · Literature snapshot: 2026-10-05

![Methods and boundaries from offline multi-teacher distillation to MOPD](../assets/method-map.svg)

Multi-teacher distillation transfers knowledge or specialized capabilities from several models into one student. Three questions make this literature easier to read: where do the training states come from, who supplies supervision, and how does that supervision update the student?

The accompanying [Awesome Multi-Teacher Distillation](../README.md) catalog contains 134 records: 63 multi-teacher on-policy works, 50 offline multi-teacher works, 13 adjacent methods, 3 single-teacher foundations, and 5 reviews or tutorials. Records include analyses and system reports, so these counts do not represent 134 distinct algorithms. The collection is a curated map, not an exhaustive systematic review.

## From fixed examples to student trajectories

Classical knowledge distillation matches a teacher's soft targets. Multiple teachers can contribute averaged predictions, task-specific supervision, or intermediate representations. Teachers can also generate demonstrations for subsequent supervised training. Offline multi-teacher distillation therefore covers more than averaging logits.

“Offline” here describes the provenance of supervised states. Targets on fixed examples may be cached or computed during training; querying a teacher live does not make those examples on-policy. [SoFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32493) constructs soft targets from fixed multi-teacher demonstrations and a frozen base distribution. [Med-RADIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37682) aligns generalist and specialist features on fixed medical images. Neither relies on current-student trajectories.

For an autoregressive language model, a state consists of a prompt and the generated prefix. The on-policy setting of [GKD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649) trains on student-generated sequences, querying a teacher at those prefixes; GKD also permits mixtures with fixed demonstrations. MOPD adds multiple independently identifiable supervisors and asks how their signals should train a shared student.

State provenance and divergence direction are separate choices. Forward KL does not define offline training, and reverse KL alone does not establish MOPD. Inspect the sampling policy and the actual update objective.

## Three tests for strict MOPD

This repository uses an explicit working definition:

1. **Student states:** Are the supervised states generated by the current or near-current student? A rollout snapshot used for its associated updates can qualify. Historical trajectories do not qualify merely because a student originally generated them.
2. **Independent supervisors:** Are there at least two identifiable teacher sources, such as separately trained expert policies? Multiple samples, role prompts, or information views from one teacher do not automatically create independent teachers. Sharing a base model does not automatically disqualify separately trained experts either.
3. **Direct objective:** Does teacher supervision directly update the student through distillation? Distribution divergences, sampled-token advantages, and feature or action targets can qualify. Inference-only ensembles, pure parameter merging, and task rewards alone are insufficient.

Answer each test separately. A hybrid pipeline may contain a qualifying MOPD stage without making every stage MOPD. Missing method details should remain uncertain rather than being inferred from a title. See the [taxonomy](taxonomy.md) for the complete rules.

## Compare mechanisms on the same axes

These are reading aids, not interchangeable implementation recipes.

| Method | State source | Teacher source | Direct objective | Purpose and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Classical KD](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531) | Fixed examples | Large model or ensemble | Temperature-softened target matching | Compression; fails the student-state test |
| [GKD, on-policy setting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649) | Current-student sequences | One teacher | Prefix-level distribution divergence | On-policy foundation; not multi-teacher; other GKD settings mix fixed and generated data |
| [MOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30406) | Current-student trajectories | Independent domain experts | Domain-routed reverse-KL supervision | Capability integration; routing does not guarantee correctness |
| [MOPD-Router](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30837) | Near-current-student trajectories | Expert pool | Routed sampled-token advantages | Teacher selection; routing metrics are not correctness proofs |
| [PMOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34605) | Current-student responses | Task experts | Reverse KL with gradient and optimizer-update projection | Interference control; evidence depends on task and schedule settings |
| [MAS-OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34234) | Current multi-agent trajectories | Role-conditioned views of one frozen teacher | Role-contrastive token advantages | Multi-agent OPD; fails the independent-supervisor test |
| [ROSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35954) | Historical student replay | Saved self-targets; one reviewer selects spans | Masked cross-entropy | Offline relearning; this stage is not strict MOPD |

MAS-OPD's multiple student agents cannot be counted as teachers: role contrast changes the context presented to the same supervisor. ROSS illustrates a different boundary. Following MOPD in an experiment does not make its relearning stage MOPD. Its reviewer selects spans, while targets remain saved trajectories. These classifications concern training mechanisms, not the value of the work. Evidence is recorded in the [adjacent catalog](../papers/adjacent-alternatives.md) and [October update](update-2026-10-05.md).

## An eight-paper reading route

Read the method and training sections with one question in mind, then examine how the experiments test it.

1. **[Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531).** What information do soft targets provide beyond hard labels? Establish the roles of temperature and ensemble compression.
2. **[Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06295).** How can several task experts train one student policy? Track which policy generates the training trajectories.
3. **[MiniLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543).** Why consider reverse KL for generative distillation? Separate objective choice, student sampling, and stabilization techniques.
4. **[On-Policy Distillation of Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649).** What changes when training uses student-generated prefixes? Keep this single-teacher foundation distinct from MOPD.
5. **[MOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30406).** How does specialize-then-unify training work? Draw the complete loop: student generation, teacher routing, scoring, and updating.
6. **[Open-MOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19098).** Do equal prompt counts imply equal training budgets? Examine response lengths, token shares, and capability imbalance.
7. **[MOPD-Router](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30837).** How can teacher assignment and weighting move beyond domain labels? Compare token-level routing evidence and additional computation.
8. **[PMOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34605).** Can updates still interfere after teacher selection? Study task-update subspaces, cycling order, and projection ablations.

This route moves from objectives and states to budgets, routing, and conflict. It is not a quality ranking. The broader [reading paths](reading-order.md) cover heterogeneous representations, vision, and agents.

## From understanding a method to evaluating adoption

Before comparing scores, check student initialization, teacher quality, prompt pools, generated-token budgets, teacher calls, and evaluation protocols. A stronger teacher pool or larger training budget can change results independently of the combination mechanism. Inspect individual domains and retained capabilities alongside averages.

Lower loss also cannot replace capability evaluation. [From Gradients to Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02179) examines loss averaging, optimizer behavior, and vocabulary restriction; closer local gradient approximation does not necessarily imply better final task performance.
